# Handover — the hex map's layout rules, 7–14 October 2026

**Written for Greg and Nate first, and for whichever agent picks this up cold.**
Everything below is on the `hex-fan-chains` branch, which sits at `origin/lab`.
`next`, `release` and `main` are untouched. Nothing is committed — see *Where the
repo is*.

---

## 1. What this was about

The map was laid out by one set of rules and then re-decided by a different set
every time anybody touched it. Greg had reported the same symptom four times
since 3 October — *"the auto-rearrange struggles and then blasts teams into
confetti"* — and the previous session diagnosed it as two engines wearing one
coat: the atlas (`territory.ts`, nested territories) deciding where things go on
load, and the drag and tidy paths (`arrange.ts`, `tidy.ts`, written when the map
was an open plane of teams) re-deciding it afterwards.

Greg took that diagnosis into a separate chat, came back with a draft rule set,
and asked for it to be played back as **a small number of ranked laws** — his
phrase was "something like Asimov's rules of robotics" — so that the governing
theory could be understood without reading code.

**The single most important output is [MAP-LAWS.md](MAP-LAWS.md).** Read it
before touching placement. The rest of this document is how it came about and
what state the code is in.

---

## 2. The laws, in one line each

Strictly ranked. A lower law yields to a higher one, always.

> **0. The hand.** Never a pinned unit. A placed unit only inside a branch
> somebody aimed tidy at. No placement improved on the engine's own initiative.
> **1. One cell, one unit.** No exceptions — a drop that cannot satisfy it is refused.
> **2. A body stays whole.** No territory broken into pieces. Pieces exist only where a person made them.
> **3. Children sit outward.** A team or above is never nearer the centre than its parent. A person is pushed outward, never forbidden.
> **4. One measure of distance.** One rule, one dial, applied on load and on tidy. The engine never imposes a second, tighter rule of its own.
> **5. Lines follow, they never lead.** Every unit gets its cell, then chains are drawn. No placement is chosen by what it does to a line.

**Laws gate. Preferences choose.** Fewer crossings, shorter chains, a neat map
and a fast layout are all preferences, and none may win an argument with a law.

The ranking is not decoration — it earned its keep twice in this session, both
times by rejecting a change whose headline number looked like a win. See §5.

---

## 3. Three findings that overturned the agreed plan

The draft rule set proposed two deletions as *"the cheapest high-value change"*.
Measured, both were wrong.

**Do not delete `groupOf` from the drop path.** Over 484 overlapping drops on the
1,000-person company, removing it turns *every* clashing drop from `parted` into
`gathered` — the branch is re-laid and whole families inside it fall from 224/248
to about 80. It is the only thing standing between a clashing drop and confetti.
It wants to *become* the territory, not to go. `gapBetween` was the harmful half
and has gone.

**`neaten` is not the confetti engine.** On the recovered gesture it *improves*
cohesion markedly — children touching their family 86.4% → 91.5%, adrift 403 →
251. Stopping it consulting routes may still be right on principle (Law 5) but it
is not a cheap win and it is not what Greg has been seeing.

**The cap of 5 never existed.** The draft said "keep the current size-proportional
gap and its cap of 5". There was no such number: the docs said `Infinity` in one
place and 5 in another, the committed code said 3, the working tree said 48.
Greg: *"the spacing gap cap at 5 is invented. We can revoke it."* The stale doc
line is deleted.

## 4. What the confetti actually is

It resisted reproduction for a week. Two mundane things hid it:

- **A drop onto empty ground is always clean** — a plain translation that cannot
  break anything. The map is now spaced so widely that an overlap has to be
  aimed for.
- **"The largest child of the root" is the wrong branch to grab.** These fixtures
  put one pass-through unit under the root, so that branch *is* the whole company
  and nothing is left outside it to collide with. Every earlier probe was
  translating into a vacuum.

The gesture was recovered from the 6 October session transcript, which had
preserved the five unit ids Greg's hand grabbed. All five are inside **Everest
Region**, whose subtree is 1,258 units — exactly the move count his saved
arrangement held. His arrangement was that one branch being moved.

**What breaks it:** dropping the node you are holding onto a tile somebody
already has. 22 of 23 such drops break the branch, against 11 of 450 when it
lands on free ground. It does not recover on a second drag, because once the
shape is gone a later clean drop faithfully translates the broken shape.

Repro: `npm run hex:confetti` (Northwind) and `app/lab/hex/confetti.test.ts`
(1,000-person, runs in the suite). Law 2 is written there as an `it.fails`, so
the suite stays green while being honest that the law is not met — **when that
test starts failing, the fix has landed and it should become `it`.**

## 5. The ranking rejecting two plausible wins

Worth keeping, because both looked like obvious improvements.

**Tidy spacing.** Letting a branch move out to the rings where its owed gap is
actually available halves the crowding — and takes whole branches from 47/58 to
**31**. Law 2 outranks Law 4, so the branch stays against its family and the room
comes from choosing which cell. Without a ranked rule set this would have
shipped.

**The halo.** Lifting the spacing cap until nothing is clipped improves curl-back
slightly and costs **5.7s → 25.9s** on Northwind. Greg chose the speed.

## 7. What is NOT working — read this first

**Greg, 14 October, after all of the above:** *"I see great progress on load
times but not on the layout / 5s placement thing."*

**This is unresolved and it is the most important open item.** The measurements
say the 5-second re-pickup went from 99% crowding to 51%, better than a clean
load, and a hand-run on Digital Tailoring gave 92% of children touching their
family and 0% curl-back. **Greg is looking at the real thing and says it is still
wrong.** When the numbers and his eyes disagree, his eyes are the authority — the
numbers have been measuring the wrong thing twice already this session (see
§8), and that is the likeliest explanation.

Do not start by re-running my probes. Start by reproducing what he sees:

1. Ask him for the exact gesture — which company, which view, which node, and
   what "pushes everything together" looks like now.
2. Check he is on current code. The panel should show a line reading
   *"N% sit nearer the centre than their parent"* and, after a tidy, *"kept the
   layout's room for N, squeezed M"*. If either is missing, he is on a stale
   build — stop the server, `rm -rf .next`, restart.
3. Note that `squeezed` is the number that announces this failure. A high
   `squeezed` means tidy wanted to give things air and could not find anywhere
   to put them.

Two candidates worth holding in mind, neither confirmed:

- **The 5s path and the tidy button take different routes.** The button runs
  `neaten` (gather, then turn). The re-pickup runs `reflow` via `placeIsland`,
  which re-lays the branch from the anchor outward. They are handed the same
  spacing now, but they are not the same algorithm, and "consistent" to Greg may
  mean the same *result*, not the same inputs.
- **`reflow` seats each child with `firstClearCell`**, which takes the nearest
  cell satisfying the gap. Nearest-that-qualifies still packs toward the parent.

## 8. Two measurement mistakes, so they are not repeated

Both flattered the answer and both were caught only because Greg's description
disagreed with the numbers.

- **"A family is a parent plus its direct children"** measures an archipelago
  property the atlas never had. What the eye reads is the **outline**, which
  wraps a unit *and all its descendants*.
- **Counting a unit touching its own grandchild as "crowding"** measures
  cohesion, not crowding. Kin is a unit's whole subtree plus its line of
  ancestors; everybody else is a stranger.

And the standing one, from 6 October: **measure what is drawn.** The panel once
reported 91% cohesion about the calculated layout while Greg was looking at his
own arrangement. Every figure on the panel is now counted from the live cells.

## 10. Next, in order

1. **Reproduce what Greg sees on the 5-second gesture** (§7). Nothing else
   matters until that is understood.
2. **Teach the drop path what a territory is** — so the body that steps aside on
   a clash is a territory at the rung you grabbed, not a flat team id. This is
   the real fix for the confetti and it makes `confetti.test.ts`'s `it.fails`
   start failing.
3. **Law 0's nuance, unbuilt.** A drag still pins its anchor automatically
   (`HexLab.tsx`, in `commit`), so tidy's power decays with every drag — which is
   exactly the objection Greg raised. The design agreed: a drag makes a
   *placement*, pinning becomes deliberate, and tidy is aimed at a branch rather
   than global.
4. **The two missing law numbers** — "placements honoured" (Law 0) and a test
   that tidy produces identical cells with routing switched off (Law 5).
5. **Lazy per-team interiors** (`hex-lazy-interiors` branch). When that lands,
   lift `MAX_HALO` and re-measure: the rule settles at 119 on its own and needs
   no cap once the search is not walking the whole company.
