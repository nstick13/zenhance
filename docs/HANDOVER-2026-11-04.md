## 1. What these two sittings were

Three faults, all of them the map not behaving the way the hand expects. None
of them was a feature; all of them were in the way of the thing Greg was
actually trying to do.

**The map moved when you built on it.** Make one node on Sparrow Jam and the
whole company re-laid itself — 2,814 × 2,837 world units became 4,452 × 4,098,
and four of the five units left the cell they were standing on. The thing you
made stayed put; everything else answered to the engine again. Greg's call on
30 October was to stop it: where everything stands now becomes the hand's
answer the moment you make something, and **tidy is the way back** — its first
press asks the engine to lay the company out again with the new thing in it,
its second throws the arrangement away. Removing a branch works the same way,
because a hole is a different tree too.

A second half of that one was missed in the first write-up and found by
measuring: how many rungs a company has grades every tile's size and shade, so
making a *person inside a team* added a rung and swelled every tile on screen
by about a third — **without anything moving**. Nodes made by hand no longer
restate the company's rungs until tidy.

**Hold-to-reparent could not be done on a big company.** Greg: *"I can reparent
a team … once. I can't then reparent. And I could only reparent to Quartz Team
50 — while the animation fired, the dialog offering reparent did not."* Three
faults under one gesture, all found by instrumenting the handler and reading
numbers out of real drags rather than by reasoning about the code:

| | what it was | at the 1,000-person company's zoom (0.02×) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| how still the hand must be | a quarter of a cell, in **world** units | **0.9 px**, for 550 ms, while dragging |
| who the hold is about | the cell under the pointer, re-decided every move | a cell is **15 px** across, so a 4 px wobble steps onto empty ground and the hold is dropped |
| when the clock started | `holdSince` on React state | the handler reads the drag its last *render* produced — measured at a flat **~1,000 ms** behind on a drag still arriving |

The third is why it felt random rather than merely hard: the release was
deciding from a clock that measured how far behind the renderer was, so
**whether the dialog appeared depended on how busy the frame was**. That is
"works once, then never", and it is why it happened on the big company.

All three fixed and driven on the company it was found on. A 40 px sweep still
arms nothing, so Greg's 2026-10-01 rule — *"pulling a family far away results
in the reparent dialog appearing"* — holds, and now holds for the right reason.

**`/org` no longer dies when a migration hasn't been run.** See section 3.

**Where to read the detail:** [HEX-LAYOUT.md](HEX-LAYOUT.md) §§ *The map no
longer flinches* and *Hold-to-reparent did not work at scale*, both with the
measurements and with what was **not** checked.

## 3. Still waiting on Greg or Nate — nobody else can do these

**One line of SQL into Neon.** The production database has never been told
about the `distance` column — the one that remembers how far from its parent
you dropped a node. It is safe to run right now; adding a column does not
disturb code that doesn't know about it, so the live site carries on exactly
as it is.

```sql
ALTER TABLE "orbital_nodes" ADD COLUMN "distance" numeric(12, 3);
```

It used to be a release blocker. It isn't any more: `/org` now notices the
column is missing and draws without it, so what is lost is a remembered
distance, not the page. **That is a seatbelt, not a licence** — migration
first, code second, still. `lib/data/missingColumn.ts` carries the reasoning,
and one finding worth keeping: Drizzle *wraps* the driver's error, so
`error.code` is `undefined` and Postgres's own `42703` sits a level down under
`cause`. A guard reading the obvious place would have done nothing.

**Five minutes in the Vercel dashboard.** [ENVIRONMENTS.md](ENVIRONMENTS.md)
flags that if preview deployments never got their own database variables, every

  `lib/map/growth/relationship.ts` is in world units, so the allowance is
  `6 × zoom` pixels. Reparenting there is gated to `scale ≥ 0.7`

## 5. For the next conversation: how the hex map looks

**This is Greg's, and it is design-first.** He parked it on 19 October with the
scope recorded and an explicit instruction not to start building from the task
entry:

> *"There's the styling of the whole thing to make the corporate layers more
> intelligible, and then there's the behaviour at each zoom level. Hex has no
> differentiation yet on either aside from a little shading and some minor node
> sizing. I have thoughts on what we should do here but it's tightly integrated
> into what shows at each level."*

He also asked for a sub-task inside it: **some styling and artistic fun**.

### What decides the look today, and where it lives

Four rules, and that is the whole of it:

| what | rule | where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **How big a node is** | a share of its cell, by rung: 85% at the centre down to 10% at the deepest, spread evenly over however many rungs the company has | `nodeAreaFraction` / `nodeScale`, `lib/map/layout/hex/scene.ts` |
| **How many rungs "however many" is** | the company the engine laid, skipping nodes a hand made here (changed 30 Oct — see below) | `maxDepth`, `HexLab.tsx` |
| **How dark a node and its wash are** | by rung, over a span that widens with the company's depth | `toneOf` / `css`, `HexLab.tsx` ~line 251 |
| **What colour a node is** | a hue per unit, stable | `hueById`, `HexLab.tsx` ~line 888 |

Plus **three greys**, which are Greg's own from 2026-10-18 and the one piece of
the look he has already specified: the hexagon spacing is *"a faint grey, just
darker than the background"*, a team's zone *"a grey between the team-zone and
the background"*, a group's ring *"a grey between the background and the team
outline"*. The governing idea is worth keeping whatever else changes —
**colour is for what is there, grey is for the room around it.** Drawn around
`HexLab.tsx` lines 1780–1810.

### A design question this work has already run into

The size-and-tone grading is **relative**: the range is stretched across
however many rungs the company happens to have. So a company that gains a rung
re-grades every tile — a division can swell by a third because somebody added a
person three levels below it. On 30 October that was stopped for nodes *made by
hand*, which was the acute case, but the underlying rule is untouched and it is
a real fork for the aesthetics conversation:

**should a team always look like a team (absolute grading), or should the
grading stretch to fit whatever the company is (relative, as now)?** Greg's
original instruction in September was explicitly relative — *"each step
calculated based on how many strata there are"* — so changing it is his call,
not a refactor.

### What hex deliberately does not have yet

The orbital map's semantic layer — three engines hex uses **none** of:
`lib/map/work` (work as a status-coloured arc resolving into a dot grid),
`lib/map/signal` (delivery, sprint and team-health rings, drawn only where real
source data exists), and the lower rungs of `lib/map/camera/lod` (people absent
at overview, then a crowd stand-in, then portraits). Plus everything in
[LARGE-COMPANY-NAVIGATION.md](LARGE-COMPANY-NAVIGATION.md) that hex has no
answer for: the local detail field, the mark budget and hierarchical thinning,
the edge basket, and focus with a route home.
