Is “on 2+ teams” a finding, a fact, or a filter?
Recommendation — it is a fact. The finding is what the fact costs. Being on several teams is structure; it only becomes something to act on when it crosses a threshold the workspace set.
Fact
A structural truth. Always true, carries no judgment, needs no threshold.
Lives as
The ghost seat. Already shipped — the map draws it.
“Grace holds a seat on 2 teams.”
Never appears in a findings rail.
Filter
A fact the user chooses to narrow the map by. Their question, not ours.
Lives as
The lens and search — not the findings engine.
“Show only people on 3+ teams.”
Zero rows is a valid answer, not an all-clear.
Finding
A fact that crossed a policy threshold and that nobody has triaged away.
Requires all three
detector fires · policy says it counts · no exception filed
“Devraj is the only Security person, across 4 teams.”
Earns a mark on the map and a card in the rail.

The split this argues for
Spread
node · hub-star
On N or more teams (default 3). The cost is context-switching, not hours.
Stat: 4 teams — a count, never a percentage.
Over-commitment
node · hub-star
Declared allocation over 100%. Someone has promised more of a person than exists.
Stat: declared 120% — the word “declared” is load-bearing.
On the “no % in findings” rule. It was right about measurement: a % is declared intent, not tracked hours. But a declared 120% is still a promise someone made. Keep the number, label it declared, and never sort by it as though it were measured.
